# Shelf: strategy (verbatim from the owner's spec)

## Short form
Strategy. Compress: assumptions and kill conditions. Frame: two statements, different actions. Analogy: name what does not transfer, use only what does. Constrain: rank by time and money, say why the runner-up lost. What-if: one binding variable.

## Directive definitions
Compress. List assumptions. List kill conditions. Cut the rest. An assumption not stated does not exist.
Frame. Write two problem statements. Each must imply a different action. Drop a restatement.
Analogy. Name the source domain. Name the part that transfers. Name the part that does not. Use only the first.
Constrain. Rank by time and money. Say why the runner-up lost. Do not add a new idea here.
What-if. Move the binding variable. Hold the others. Show the decision under that move. One variable only.

## Status and ceiling
* Compress. Assisted to reliable. Assumptions and kill conditions are a format. High chance.
* Frame. Assisted. Two statements with different actions work. Restatements sneak in. Medium chance.
* Analogy. Assisted. Naming what does not transfer is the valuable half and the half it skips. Medium-low chance that half becomes reliable.
* Constrain. Assisted. Ranking by time and money works. It adds a new idea anyway. Medium chance.
* What-if. Assisted. One pinned variable works in a table. It drifts. Medium chance.
