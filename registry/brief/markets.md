# Shelf: markets (markets research; verbatim from the owner's spec)

## Short form
Markets. Print: EPS and guidance from the release, one surprise, numbers match. Filing: quote, page or item, no recap, skip what you cannot place. Cross: both lines, mark agreement, no merged voice. Memo: thesis, observable kill condition, fact versus inference, no order or size. Monitor: delta, expire, keep kill condition. Scenario: one variable, others fixed.
Now: reliable if document-locked. No broker tool.

## Directive definitions
Print. Take EPS from the release. Take guidance from the release. Name one surprise. Each number matches the document.
Filing. Quote the line. Record the page or item. Do not use the news recap as the filing. Skip a figure you cannot place.
Cross. Show the filing line. Show the news line. Mark agreement or not. Do not merge them into one voice.
Memo. State the thesis. State the kill condition as an observable. Separate fact from inference. No order, no size.
Monitor. Compare to the last card. Expire stale claims. Add only the delta. Keep the kill condition in view.
Scenario. Move one variable. Hold the others fixed. Show the thesis under that move. Stop after that variable.

## Status and ceiling
No public benchmark matches these directives. Mechanism is extraction from a filing plus a refusal to trade.
* Print. Reliable if the release is the input. It grabs the recap number if the recap is closer. High chance a document-lock makes it reliable.
* Filing. Assisted. Page and quote work on text filings. Tables and exhibits slip. Medium chance.
* Cross. Assisted. Side-by-side works. Merged voice is the failure. Medium chance.
* Memo. Assisted. Thesis plus a kill condition is writable. The kill condition is often not observable. Medium chance.
* Monitor. Assisted with a file and a clock. High chance, same reason as research monitor.
* Scenario. Assisted. One-variable moves work if the others are pinned in the prompt. It moves two. Medium chance. Gate on orders stays. Low chance a model is allowed to place risk by 2027 in any serious desk.
